04 · How a real audit runs

Eight stages, from mandate to follow-up

This is the sequence a security audit of a data centre follows, whoever runs it. I have not led one. I have studied each stage and I work inside the controls these audits test.

CertificationISO/IEC 27001Stage 1 reviews the documented ISMS. Stage 2 tests that it operates. Surveillance audits follow each year, with recertification every three years.
AttestationSOC 2 Type IIAn independent auditor tests controls over a period, often six to twelve months, and issues an opinion with every test and exception listed.
CompliancePCI DSS Requirement 9Where a site hosts cardholder data, an assessor tests physical access to it: entry controls, visitor handling and media.
Internal and customerClause 9.2 and right to auditThe operator audits itself on a planned programme. Tenants audit the operator under their contracts.

CriteriaC2. A contractor's induction must be current on the day of access.
ConditionM. Tavita was issued card V-017 at 09:10 on 16 Nov. The induction register shows expiry on 2 Nov.
CauseNot yet established. A hypothesis to test: expiry dates are not visible at the point a card is issued.
ConsequenceA person without current site safety and security briefing worked in a controlled area.
Corrective actionBlock card issue when induction has lapsed. Owner and due date to be agreed with site management.

Four exceptions in eight rows says something about this day. It says little about the year. A conclusion on whether the control operated over a period needs a sample drawn across that period, which is the difference between a SOC 2 Type I and a Type II report.

    Penetration testing · the governance side

    A test is only as useful as what happens to its findings

    I do not perform penetration tests. Two of the risks above start as test findings, PT-01 and PT-02. My part is to see that a test is authorised and scoped, and that each finding gets an owner and a retest.

    Before

    Rules of engagement

    • Written authorisation from someone with authority over the site
    • Scope, exclusions and techniques agreed in advance
    • A time window, emergency contacts and a stop word
    • Safety limits: no forced entry, nothing that touches power or life safety
    During

    What a physical test tries

    • Following staff through a controlled door
    • Talking past the front desk with a cover story
    • Using a found, borrowed or copied access card
    • Looking for unlocked racks, rooms and screens
    After

    Where governance starts

    • Each finding is mapped to the control that failed
    • It enters the risk register with a rating and a named owner
    • A corrective action is agreed with a due date
    • The fix is retested before the finding is closed
